Reading the quadrant. A release ratio near 8–13% is the normal run-off pace for a stable book. Far above it means the store is being drawn down quickly relative to what remains; far below it means a long runway. NB/release below 1.0 for more than a year or two is the early warning on future earnings.

CHUBB and Rabbit Life sit off the top of the scale for a reason. Both took heavy onerous-contract charges in FY2024 — a restated TFRS 17 transition comparative, not recognised performance — which suppressed the FY2025 release and inflates the ratio. Read their position as transition mechanics, not as unusually strong new business.

T Life’s CSM interest accretion is negative in FY2025 (−5.9m against +7.9m in FY2024). Accretion at locked-in rates on a positive CSM should be positive; the likely explanation is that something else is netted into that row. The roll-forward closes exactly with it as printed, so it is carried unchanged.
